How to Set Up an American Mahjong Game: Wash, Walls, Dice and the Deal

Setup takes about three minutes once everyone knows the routine: wash the tiles, build four walls, roll for East, break the wall, and deal until East has 14 tiles and everyone else has 13.

Updated September 20267 min readMahjongKit Editorial Team

Quick answerEach player starts with 13 tiles, and East (the dealer) starts with 14. Walls are 19 stacks long and two tiles high, because an American set has 152 tiles.

What you need

  • An American Mahjong set: 152 tiles including 8 Jokers (most sets add blank spares)
  • Four racks, ideally with pushers
  • Two dice
  • A current NMJL card for each player
  • A square table, and ideally a mat to quiet the tiles

If you are still choosing equipment, our set buying guide explains what to look for.

1. Wash the tiles

Turn all 152 tiles face down in the middle of the table. Everyone uses both hands to swirl them around for fifteen or twenty seconds. This is the wash, and the clatter is part of the game's charm. Keep going until nobody could possibly know where any tile is.

2. Build the walls

Each player builds one side of a square in front of their own rack.

  1. Lay a row of 19 tiles

    Slide face-down tiles from the middle into a straight line in front of your rack until it is 19 tiles long.

  2. Stack a second row on top

    Build a second row of 19 and lift it onto the first. Your wall is now 19 stacks long and two tiles high.

  3. Push the wall forward

    Use the pusher on your rack (or your hands) to slide the wall toward the center so the four walls form a square.

All 152 tiles are now in the walls (4 × 19 × 2 = 152). If a wall is short or long, count and fix it before rolling.

Different game, different wall. In traditional Chinese Mahjong the set has 144 tiles, so walls are 18 stacks long. If you sit down at a table with 18-stack walls, you are probably not playing American rules. See Chinese vs. American Mahjong.

3. Choose East (the dealer)

The dealer is called East. For the first hand, each player rolls both dice and the highest total becomes East. After that, East usually passes to the right after each hand, though many groups let the winner or a fixed rotation decide. East has a small edge: they roll to break the wall and begin with 14 tiles.

4. Break the wall

East rolls both dice again, and the total says where to break East's own wall.

  1. Count stacks from East's right

    East counts that many stacks from the right-hand end of East's own wall. A roll of 8 means the eighth stack.

  2. Break it there

    East opens a gap after that stack and pushes the right-hand end of the wall out towards the middle of the table, so everyone can reach it.

  3. Select from the break

    Tiles are taken from the break and along the wall to the left. If East's wall runs out, the player to East's left pushes out their own wall and selection carries on from it.

5. Deal the tiles

East takes the first two stacks (four tiles). The player to East's right takes the next two stacks, then the player across, then the player to East's left. Go around three times so that everyone has 12 tiles.

Then the final pick: East takes two more tiles (traditionally the top tiles of the first and third remaining stacks), and each of the other three players takes one. East now has 14 tiles and everyone else has 13.

Rack your tiles, keep them hidden, and move straight into the Charleston. During play, East discards first to bring their hand down to 13, and each turn afterwards is draw one, discard one.

Beginner tipCount your tiles the moment the deal ends and again after the Charleston. A wrong tile count is the most common way a beginner ends up with a dead hand.

Setup questions

How many tiles does each player start with?

13 tiles. East, the dealer, starts with 14 and discards first.

How long is an American Mahjong wall?

19 stacks long and two tiles high on each of the four sides, for 152 tiles total.

Who starts as East?

For the first hand, whoever rolls highest with two dice. Groups vary on how East moves after that; passing to the right each hand is common.

Which direction does play go?

Counter-clockwise: after East comes the player on East's right.

Can you play American Mahjong with three players?

Yes, with house rules. Common adjustments include leaving one wall's tiles in reserve and adapting the Charleston (across passes have no partner, so they are skipped or swapped for a different pass). There is no single official three-player standard, so agree the rules first.

Official rules note. This guide explains American Mahjong as it is commonly played with the National Mah Jongg League card. House rules and tournament procedures can vary.
