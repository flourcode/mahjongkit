Where to Play American Mahjong Online (NMJL Rules)

Most "Mahjong" apps are solitaire or Chinese-rules games. A handful play real American Mahjong with the card, Jokers and the Charleston. These are the ones worth knowing.

Updated September 20267 min readMahjongKit Editorial Team

On this page

  2. Real Mah Jongg
  3. I Love Mahj
  4. Mahjong Time
  5. MyJongg.net
  6. Solo practice apps
  7. Which one to pick

Time-sensitive. Features, subscriptions and prices change often. The details below were checked in September 2026. Confirm on each platform's own site before you pay for anything.

Playing online is the fastest way to learn a new card, practice the Charleston and get in reps when you cannot gather four people. Unlike Chinese and Riichi Mahjong, which have hundreds of video games, American Mahjong is served by a small number of dedicated platforms. Here is what each one is good for.

NMJL Online (the League's official game)

The National Mah Jongg League now publishes its own online game, NMJL Online, available as an app and on the web. To play you need the current physical card plus an online game membership from the League. It includes an interactive tutorial (playable without a membership), a lobby for live games and a range of computer opponents that can also take over if a player drops out.

  • Good for: playing strictly by the official rules with the official card.
  • Keep in mind: requires buying the card and a membership through the League.

Real Mah Jongg

A long-running app (iOS, Android and web) that plays American Mahjong with the NMJL card and also supports some alternative cards. You can play against live players, bots, or a mix, invite friends to private tables, use hand suggestions while you learn, and chat by voice or text. At the time of writing the app offers a two-week free trial and then a monthly subscription (about $6.99/month in the App Store).

  • Good for: a large player pool, mobile play, learning the card with suggestions turned on.
  • Keep in mind: subscription required after the trial.

I Love Mahj

A browser-based platform built by a Mahjong-playing couple that started as a tool for organizing in-person games and grew into an online game, learning tools and a player finder. It supports the NMJL card and also offers its own free, timeless practice card so newcomers can start before their NMJL card arrives. You can play with bots, with strangers or at private tables with friends. Pricing is subscription-based; check the site for current plans.

  • Good for: beginners who want statistics and practice tools, and groups that want private tables.
  • Keep in mind: browser-based rather than a native phone app.

Mahjong Time

A large multi-ruleset platform that offers American Mahjong alongside Chinese Official, Riichi and other variants, with a 3D table view and online tournaments. Membership tiers are subscription-based.

  • Good for: players who also want other variants, and competitive online tournaments.
  • Keep in mind: the interface and virtual-currency system are more complex than the American-only platforms.

MyJongg.net

An independent, lightweight browser game with a simple top-down table that follows NMJL rules. It runs well on older computers and tablets and has a small, friendly community. A limited free tier and a low-cost premium option have historically been available.

  • Good for: a no-frills, low-cost place to play.
  • Keep in mind: a smaller player base can mean waiting for a table of live players.

Solo practice apps

If you mainly want to drill the card, several phone apps let you play solo hands against bots, replay decisions and track which hands you complete. Look for apps that name the current card year explicitly and that list NMJL rules in the description; pricing is typically a modest one-time purchase or a small monthly subscription.
