The Best American Mahjong Sets: What to Look For and Well-Regarded Picks

A regulation American set needs 152 tiles including 8 Jokers, four racks and pushers. After that it comes down to readability, material, case and price. Here is how to choose.

Updated September 20268 min readMahjongKit Editorial Team

Quick answerMake sure any set you buy has at least 152 tiles including 8 Jokers, four racks and four pushers. A 144-tile set is a Chinese set and cannot be used for NMJL play.

How we chose

We have not physically tested every set on this page. We researched and compared sets on the features that matter at a real table: tile count and Jokers, readability of the tile faces, material, rack and pusher design, the case, portability and value, alongside the consensus in player communities. Treat the picks as a shortlist, and check the listing for exact contents before you buy.

What a regulation set needs

  • 152 tiles minimum, including 8 Jokers. Most American sets ship with 166 tiles: the 152 you play with, plus spare Flowers, spare Jokers and a few blanks that can replace a lost tile. Do not buy a 144-tile set for American play; those are Chinese sets without Jokers.
  • Four racks with pushers. Racks hold your tiles upright and hidden. Pushers are the arms that slide your wall into the middle. Sets without pushers are frustrating; "all-in-one" racks with a permanently attached pusher are the most popular design because there is nothing to lose or assemble.
  • Dice. Two is enough. Some sets add a wind indicator or betting chips, which are nice but optional.

What actually matters when you compare sets

Readability

Look for large, high-contrast numerals on the Bams, Craks and Dots. Many sets print Arabic numerals in the corner alongside the traditional symbol, which speeds up beginners and helps anyone playing under dim lamps.

Material

Melamine is the classic choice: dense, opaque, durable and with the traditional clack. Acrylic sets are lighter, allow decorative tile backs and dominate the designer end of the market. Our tile materials guide goes deeper.

Tile size

Standard American tiles are a little larger than most Chinese tiles. Bigger tiles are easier to read and handle but need a bigger table. If you have a small card table, check the set's tile dimensions and add up 19 tiles per wall.

The case

Soft fabric or leatherette bags are lighter and easier to carry to a friend's house. Aluminum attaché cases protect the tiles best and look traditional but weigh more.

Well-regarded picks

Good first set

Yellow Mountain Imports American Mahjong Set (soft case)

A widely recommended starter set with melamine tiles, easy-to-read faces and a padded fabric carrying bag.

  • Typically 166 tiles including Jokers and spares, with wooden racks, pushers, dice and a wind indicator
  • Large numerals alongside the traditional symbols
  • Lighter to carry than an aluminum case

Approximate price: mid-range; check current listings.

Designer look

Yellow Mountain Imports "Chinoise" set

The same maker's decorative option, with blue-and-white patterned tile backs and a leatherette case, for players who want a set that looks like a gift.

  • Acrylic tiles with patterned backs
  • Premium soft case
  • Faces remain plain and readable

Approximate price: upper mid-range; check current listings.

Hard case and durability

White Swan Mah Jongg set (white tiles)

A traditional aluminum attaché case set aimed at frequent hosts, with sturdy latches, padded trays and crisp engraving.

  • Aluminum case with compartments for the racks and pushers
  • Color-coordinated all-in-one pushers
  • Large, smooth tiles with deep engraving

Approximate price: premium; check current listings.

Budget

Zenstyle American Mahjong set

An inexpensive complete set that still includes the full tile count and all-in-one racks.

  • 166 melamine tiles including Jokers
  • Racks with attached pushers
  • Often the cheapest way to get a regulation set

Approximate price: entry level; check current listings.

Before you buy

  • Count the Jokers in the listing. Eight is the standard; a set with none is not an American set.
  • Check whether pushers are included, not just racks.
  • Read recent buyer photos for tile clarity; product renders can be flattering.
  • Buy the current NMJL card separately from the League. Sets do not include it.
